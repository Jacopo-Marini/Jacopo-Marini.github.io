The Innovation Cost of Mergers
Abstract

Rising market concentration and rents have long been viewed as potential drivers of the slowdown in U.S. innovation. This paper studies whether mergers have contributed to that decline. Using public-company mergers, I show that M&A activity significantly reduces innovative output. Firms exposed to a rival’s merger produce fewer and less valuable innovations, with effects persisting for several years. These losses don’t seem to be offset by the merging firms’ response: at the industry level, merger waves are followed by sustained declines in innovative activity. Rivals’ markups decrease, pointing away from simple interpretations based on the anti-competitive effects of mergers. The findings challenge the widespread view that U.S. antitrust policy is adequately set up to foster innovation.
